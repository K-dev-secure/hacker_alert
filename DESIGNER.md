# Hacker Alert : cartes pour le designer

## Cartes rouges : niveaux Junior et Pro

**#1 · Spot the trap** (Phishing)  
Dos : A text says your parcel is on hold: pay €1.99 by a link. What do you do?

**#2 · Spot the trap** (Phishing)  
Dos : Your "boss" writes from a free email address and asks you to buy gift cards secretly. What do you do?

**#3 · Free smartphone** (Phishing)  
Dos : You already clicked the link and typed your password. What now?

**#4 · Strange attachment** (Phishing)  
Dos : A colleague already opened invoice.pdf.exe. What do you do first?

**#5 · Holiday dates** (Phishing)  
Dos : The same email now asks for your password to open the intranet. What do you do?

**#6 · Card blocked** (Phishing)  
Dos : The text gives a phone number. Which number do you call?

**#7 · Password request** (Passwords)  
Dos : An email asks you to reply with your password to keep your mailbox. What do you do?

**#8 · Password request** (Passwords)  
Dos : IT asks you to choose a new password. Which card helps you most?

**#9 · Easy password** (Passwords)  
Dos : The accountant can't remember long passwords. Which card helps him?

**#10 · Same password everywhere** (Passwords)  
Dos : Netflix leaked his password. What still protects his bank account?

**#11 · Stolen password** (Passwords)  
Dos : The hacker also needs a code sent to the boss's phone. Which card made that happen?

**#12 · Post-it password** (Passwords)  
Dos : The intern leaves his desk with his session open. What should he do?

**#13 · Messy desk** (Devices)  
Dos : A 2FA code is visible on a phone left on the desk. What should the owner do?

**#14 · Strange screen** (Devices)  
Dos : Your files are locked and a ransom is asked. Which card gets them back without paying?

**#15 · USB key in the car park** (Devices)  
Dos : Your colleague already plugged the key in. What now?

**#16 · Video player update** (Devices)  
Dos : How do you get a real update for your video player?

**#17 · Lunch break** (Devices)  
Dos : You come back and see someone at your computer. What do you do?

**#18 · Old laptop** (Devices)  
Dos : Hackers used the flaw and your files are gone. What saves you?

**#19 · Incoming call** (Social engineering)  
Dos : A caller from "IT" asks for the code you just received. What do you do?

**#20 · Incoming call** (Social engineering)  
Dos : Your "bank" calls and asks you to confirm a payment in your app. What do you do?

**#21 · Urgent invoice** (Social engineering)  
Dos : The invoice was already paid. What do you do at once?

**#22 · Delivery man** (Social engineering)  
Dos : He says he forgot his badge and shows a delivery note. What do you do?

**#23 · Meeting moved** (Social engineering)  
Dos : The same manager now calls from an unknown number and asks for a bank transfer. What do you do?

**#24 · Curious journalist** (Social engineering)  
Dos : He now sends an email with a "list to complete" attached. What do you do?

**#25 · Safe or fake link?** (Network)  
Dos : A QR code on a poster opens a login page. What do you do before typing?

**#26 · Safe or fake link?** (Network)  
Dos : At a café, two Wi-Fi networks have almost the same name. What do you do?

**#27 · Station Wi-Fi** (Network)  
Dos : You really must send the file now. What is the safe way?

**#28 · No padlock** (Network)  
Dos : Now the site has a padlock, but the address is bluestonebank.com.secure-check.net. What do you do?

**#29 · Update tonight** (Network)  
Dos : After the update, a pop-up asks you to "confirm your password" on an outside website. What do you do?

**#30 · Ransomware!** (Network)  
Dos : How do you make sure this can't stop the company again next month?

## Cartes rouges : niveau Expert

**#31 · Failed checks** (Phishing)  
Texte : An invoice email from billing@novacorp.com. The headers show: SPF fail, DKIM none, DMARC fail. The Reply-To is pay@novacorp-billing.example.  
Dos : The same fake email reached 50 people. Which card protects everyone?

**#32 · Lookalike domain** (Phishing)  
Texte : A login page asks for your NovaCorp password. The address bar shows xn--nvacorp-9ig.com: the "o" in "nоvacorp" is a Cyrillic letter.  
Dos : A colleague already typed his password on that page. What do you do first?

**#33 · Password manager rollout** (Phishing)  
Texte : An email from it@novacorp.com (SPF, DKIM and DMARC: pass) announces the new company password manager. The link goes to intranet.novacorp.com.  
Dos : The link now goes to novacorp-passwords.com instead of intranet.novacorp.com. What do you do?

**#34 · MFA fatigue** (Passwords)  
Texte : At 2 am, the CFO receives 15 "Approve sign-in?" notifications on her phone. She did not try to log in.  
Dos : The CFO already approved one request by mistake. What do you do first?

**#35 · Brute force** (Passwords)  
Texte : The logs show 400 failed logins on the "admin" account, then one successful login from an unknown IP abroad (203.0.113.45).  
Dos : The attacker's IP keeps trying on other accounts. What do you do?

**#36 · Encryption in progress** (Devices)  
Texte : Security alert: a laptop is encrypting files on the shared drive right now.  
Dos : The laptop is isolated. The shared files are encrypted. How do you get them back?

**#37 · Deepfake CEO** (Social engineering)  
Texte : The CFO gets a video call from the "CEO". The face and voice look real. He asks for an urgent and secret €80,000 transfer.  
Dos : The CEO's real number doesn't answer and the "CEO" says it's urgent. What do you do?

**#38 · Open door** (Network)  
Texte : An Nmap scan of NovaCorp's public IP shows: 3389/tcp open ms-wbt-server (Remote Desktop) and 445/tcp open microsoft-ds (file sharing).  
Dos : The ports are closed, but staff still need remote access from home. What protects the VPN login?

**#39 · VPN flaw** (Network)  
Texte : CERT-FR warns: a critical flaw in the brand of VPN NovaCorp uses is being actively exploited. A patch is available.  
Dos : The patch is installed, but logs show a VPN login from an unknown IP last night. What do you do?

## Cartes vertes : tous niveaux

**#0 · It's safe!**  
Texte : The message is real: right address, nothing asked, no pressure. Carry on.  
Dos : ALWAYS AVAILABLE

**#1 · Check the sender**  
Texte : Look at the real address or number before you trust a message.  
Dos : USED

**#2 · Don't click**  
Texte : Don't open the link or the attachment.  
Dos : USED

**#3 · Strong password**  
Texte : 12+ characters or 4 random words, never "123456".  
Dos : USED

**#4 · Password manager**  
Texte : A safe that remembers a different password for every account.  
Dos : USED

**#5 · Two-factor (2FA)**  
Texte : A second lock: a code sent to your phone.  
Dos : USED

**#6 · Update**  
Texte : Install real updates: they repair security holes.  
Dos : USED

**#7 · Backup**  
Texte : A copy of your files, kept somewhere else.  
Dos : USED

**#8 · Lock your screen**  
Texte : Windows + L every time you leave your desk.  
Dos : USED

**#9 · Call back to check**  
Texte : Call the person back on a number you already know.  
Dos : USED

**#10 · Avoid public Wi-Fi**  
Texte : Use your phone's connection, not free Wi-Fi.  
Dos : USED

**#11 · Antivirus scan**  
Texte : Scan the device or the file before using it.  
Dos : USED

**#12 · Alert IT**  
Texte : Tell the IT team now. Never hide a problem.  
Dos : USED

## Cartes vertes Expert (en orange)

**#13 · Isolate the machine**  
Texte : Cut it from the network (cable and Wi-Fi), but don't switch it off.  
Dos : USED

**#14 · Patch the server**  
Texte : Install the security fix now, before hackers use the flaw.  
Dos : USED

**#15 · Block the access**  
Texte : Close the port or block the address on the firewall.  
Dos : USED

**#16 · Reset & revoke**  
Texte : Change the password and log out all open sessions.  
Dos : USED
